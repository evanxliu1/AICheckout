## Terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Site | A registrable domain from the [retail frame](#retail-frame). The unit of sampling, splitting and site-level bounds |
| Home list | The CrUX country list a site is ranked in for the frame: the U.S. list for region `US`, otherwise its region's list |
| Band | A site's CrUX rank bucket in its home list: `top-1k` (ranks 1–1,000), `1k-5k` (1,001–5,000) or `5k-10k` (5,001–10,000) |
| Family | A site's retailer brand family in the frame (`amazon` for amazon.de and amazon.co.uk). At most one eligible storefront per family per country |
| Operator | The company that runs a site's online store, from the frame (`williams-sonoma-inc` for potterybarn.com and westelm.com; `amazon` for every Amazon storefront). An operator never spans two splits |
| Region group | A site's frame attribute: `us`, `canada-latam`, `europe`, `asia-pacific` or `middle-east-africa`, from the storefront region the frame predicts. Never taken from a page |
| Stream | `us` (region group `us`) or `non-us` (every other group). Each stream has its own candidates, visit order and stop rule |
| Page-state | One snapshot of one site in one [state](#states), for example `gap.com/cart-1`. The unit of labelling and page-level bounds |
| Real page-state | A snapshot captured from the live site (action states, with their observed tags) |
| Variant | A page-state made offline by a [transform](#offline-variants) of a real snapshot. It is never a real page-state |
| Split | `development`, `heldout-a` or `heldout-b`. A site and all its page-states and variants belong to exactly one split |
| Reader run | Any execution of reader code over any snapshot of a split, including partial, timing-only or debugging runs |
| Active held-out split | `heldout-a` until it is retired, then `heldout-b` |

## Retail frame

The reader evaluation draws every sample from **`retail-frame.3`**: `evals/merchants/retail-frame-3.json` (2,890 domains, 1,861 eligible, **SHA-256 `cdd1695bf99a8c14f3aed23f38be9c911a121c36d2be70f80b2058896546b28e`**). It is the most-visited online retailers by Chrome users, per country. It carries each domain's CrUX rank **bucket** per list, as CrUX publishes it; CrUX has no exact ranks.

`retail-frame.1` (`evals/merchants/retail-frame.json`, 670 domains, 457 eligible, SHA-256 `e513982616d9592c464f66dd085a0e004fce11ad84ca730af8d0a7fe14356886`) stays frozen and unchanged. It is not a reader frame; it remains the population of the [merchant-pipeline held-out list](#merchant-pipeline-held-out-domains). `retail-frame.2` (`evals/merchants/retail-frame-2.json`, Tranco 647LX, 989 eligible, SHA-256 `659dae60bbabfb0b2a392a247da13f1d8b7547c7e36ad206704b1d778eb75312`) stays committed and unchanged but is **superseded**: no selection reads it.

- **Ranking source.** The [Chrome UX Report](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/crux) (CrUX) top lists for month **2026-08** (`yyyymm` 202608, the latest published on 2026-10-05): the global list and the country lists of the **U.S. and 24 other countries**, GB, DE, FR, IT, ES, NL, PL, SE, TR (Europe), JP, KR, CN, IN, ID, AU (Asia-Pacific), CA, MX, BR, AR (Canada and Latin America), SA, AE, ZA, NG and EG (Middle East and Africa), chosen to cover the largest e-commerce markets in every region. CrUX ranks web **origins** by completed page loads of Chrome users who opted in to usage statistics, in rank-magnitude buckets (1,000, 5,000, 10,000, 50,000 and so on), in random order within a bucket. The lists were downloaded as cached by [zakird/crux-top-lists](https://github.com/zakird/crux-top-lists) (`data/global/202608.csv.gz`, `data/country/<cc>/202608.csv.gz`) into the gitignored `evals/merchants/data/crux/`, with every file's SHA-256 pinned in the build script and in the frame (`lists.sha256`); for example the U.S. list is `9e7377a7e0aedc3fdc9a579ccfef006566b172ef2687ac9928f687932c5c1a20` and the global list `4ae541994a7e28f2a9ddd33f711effde9346c4128c506782fa77c268d8f3905b`. The 25 country lists of **2026-02** and **2026-05** (`yyyymm` 202602 and 202605) are downloaded and pinned the same way (`lists.persistence`) for the persistence rule; the U.S. ones are `2a88ed7c7a871d1decb06ff1048c1740692baee9529b87c78157051e97cbe2aa` and `f6eacb531ab63217d15c2b39fd31c3a20db8bb5fd58522cfbcc473b76fe5741b`.
- **What was classified.** Every origin in the U.S. list to bucket 5,000 (ranks 1–5,000) and in each other country list to bucket 1,000: 29,000 list rows, 21,593 distinct hosts. The U.S. needs the second bucket to fill its 200 candidates; every other stream is filled from the first bucket. Bucket `5k-10k` exists as a band name but is not classified, so no site has it.
- **Classification.** Nine claude-code/claude-opus-5-5 subagents classified the hosts in parallel batches on 2026-10-05 from knowledge, **without visiting any website**, under one committed rule sheet (`evals/merchants/frame-3-inputs/classification-rules.txt`). Each listed the **storefront hosts** of online sellers of physical goods (the hosts that serve home, search, product or cart pages, not seller, help, account or payment hosts) with a code, region, currency and family; everything else was left out as not retail. Their unedited output is `frame-3-inputs/classified-hosts.tsv` (3,101 hosts). The builder reconciled it in `frame-3-inputs/reconcile.tsv` (one classification per registrable domain, consistent family slugs, the rules unified across batches, the review's exclusions, and one storefront the batches missed, hottopic.com, added; each row kind and the reasons are in the file), leaving 3,032 storefront hosts. The rules the builder added after the review are appended to the rule sheet. Eligible means, as in frame 2, an online storefront that sells physical goods to consumers with its own checkout, in any country and currency; marketplaces stay eligible.
- **Region, currency, home list and band.** `region` (ISO 3166-1 alpha-2), `currency` (ISO 4217) and `regionGroup` describe the storefront a visitor **in the United States** gets at the domain without choosing a country, as in frame 2. A site's **home list** is the U.S. list for region `US`, otherwise its region's list, and its **band** is the best bucket of its storefront hosts in its home list. A domain also carries `lists` (its bucket in every chosen list where it was classified), `globalBucket` (its bucket in the global list), `family`, `operator`, `hosts` and `entryHost`: its best-ranked storefront host in its home list, ties broken by the order of `hosts` (see [capture posture](#capture-posture)). A global brand whose U.S. visitors get a U.S. store (zara.com, ikea.com) is a U.S. site, ranked by the U.S. list. Country storefronts on subdomains of a global domain (kr.iherb.com, uk.pandora.net) are folded into the domain's U.S. storefront, so they never enter the non-U.S. stream.
- **Persistence (objective, blind to pages).** A storefront is eligible only if, in **at least one** of the two earlier lists of its home country (2026-02 or 2026-05), one of its storefront hosts, **or the same host with or without a `www.`, `m.` or `mobile.` label**, is within the home list's classified depth (U.S. ranks 1–5,000, others 1–1,000). No other subdomain counts: an account, seller, payment or blog host of the same domain never makes a storefront persistent. Otherwise the site is `not-persistently-popular`. The rule is applied before the family rule. It excludes seasonal retailers, for example spirithalloween.com, which is popular only around Halloween.
- **One storefront per retailer family per country.** Among a family's eligible domains with the same region, the one in the best band is kept, then the lowest SHA-256 key of purpose `retailer-family`; the others are `same-retailer-other-domain` (41). Sister storefronts in different countries (amazon.de, amazon.fr) stay eligible.
- **Operator.** `operator` comes from the committed table `frame-3-inputs/operators.tsv`: by family, with domain overrides (for example Bloomingdale's UAE is run by Al Tayer, not Macy's), and the family slug by default. Its rule is in the rule sheet: the company that runs the online store; a holding or private-equity owner whose brands run their own stores is not an operator; when unsure, group. 1,861 eligible sites have 1,421 operators. The [split](#splits-defined-now-applied-in-123) keeps an operator in one split, which protects what frame 2's one-per-family rule protected: no shared store code in development and a held-out split at once.
- **Exclusion codes.** Frame 2's codes keep their meaning: `legacy-named-merchant` (amazon.com, bestbuy.com, newegg.com only; Amazon's other country domains are eligible), `not-a-store` (also classifieds, price comparison, coupon and cashback sites, vehicle and live-animal sellers), `mostly-digital-goods`, `cross-border-marketplace` (Temu, Shein, AliExpress, DHgate, LightInTheBox, Joom; single-brand direct-to-consumer stores are not cross-border), `prescription-or-pharmacy` (also prescription eyewear and contact lenses), `no-own-checkout`, `business-supplier`, `registry-service`, `duplicate-of-another-domain`, `multi-market-domain`, `card-unusable-market` (Russia and Belarus, and Iran), `adult-retailer` and `same-retailer-other-domain`. `not-a-store` also covers storefront-hosting platforms (myeasyorders.com, shopier.com) and `no-own-checkout` Instacart-run grocers (publix.com). New in frame 3:
  - `sensitive-goods` (31): stores selling mostly firearms and ammunition (suppressors included), tobacco, snus and vape, cannabis or alcohol (state liquor boards included), where no eligible item exists.
  - `no-fixed-price-cart-or-members-only` (57): auctions, multi-level-marketing and consultant shops, members-only or verified-group stores, flash-sale clubs that need sign-in (Veepee, Showroomprivé, Privalia, Zalando Privé, BestSecret, limango, Rue La La, Gilt), lease-to-own, pledges, duty-free pre-orders and lotteries. eBay and the warehouse clubs (Costco, Sam's Club, BJ's) stay eligible.
  - `carrier-device-shop` (9): a mobile carrier's device shop, in every country (Orange, Movistar, Claro, MTN, Vodafone, Zain).
  - `not-persistently-popular` (177): fails the persistence rule above.
  - `outside-home-list` (19): an eligible storefront not in the classified part of its home list (for example a U.S. storefront seen only in another country's top 1,000).
  - `region-not-in-country-set` (6): an eligible storefront whose region is none of the 25 countries.
  - `outside-top-100k` and `defunct-2025` don't occur: every frame domain is in a list.
- **Counts.** Eligible: `us` 99 `top-1k` and 326 `1k-5k` (425); `non-us` 1,436 `top-1k` in 24 countries. Per country (`top-1k`): GB 92, DE 103, FR 90, IT 70, ES 63, NL 114, PL 106, SE 114, TR 98, JP 63, KR 28, CN 4, IN 24, ID 5, AU 97, CA 73, MX 53, BR 43, AR 56, SA 15, AE 27, ZA 64, NG 1, EG 33. By region group: `europe` 850, `asia-pacific` 221, `canada-latam` 225, `middle-east-africa` 140, `us` 425. 21 currencies (20 besides USD), all in the minor-unit table and the item price bands. Excluded: 1,029, by code in the frame's `counts`.
- **Frozen inputs.** `reconcile.tsv` and `operators.tsv` are frozen with this protocol, like the frame: they change only by a dated amendment, which reports both frames.
- **Known bias.** CrUX counts page loads of **Chrome users who opted in** to usage statistics, so browsers other than Chrome, app traffic (much of the shopping on Coupang, Shopee and Mercado Libre) and Chrome users who opted out are not counted; that is a good match for the extension's users, who shop in desktop Chrome, and a poor one for a country's whole retail market. Chrome's share is low in China (only 4 Chinese storefronts reach CN's top 1,000) and app-first markets (Indonesia 5, Nigeria 1). The persistence rule favours long-standing retailers over new ones and **excludes seasonal retailers** whose traffic peaks in one season (spirithalloween.com), so seasonal shopping is under-represented. **Buckets are coarse**: within a bucket the order is random, so "most popular" means "in the most popular bucket", not a ranking. Retailers are ranked by **origin**: a retailer spread over many hosts may rank lower than its traffic. The **classification is a model's**, from knowledge, without visiting sites: it misses shops it doesn't know, and dozens of little-known small sites were included on their names alone (the batches' uncertain hosts are listed in the decision record); capture excludes a non-store found there (`not-a-store`, `defunct`). Region and currency are predictions for a **U.S. vantage point**, as in frame 2: some non-U.S. sites geo-price, show a cross-border storefront or refuse U.S. visitors (`geo-blocked`). Results generalize to "the most-visited retail sites among Chrome users in the U.S. and 24 large markets, as seen from a U.S. connection", and every report MUST say so.

## Seeded selection

Every random choice in this protocol is a deterministic order. A domain's key is the lowercase hex SHA-256 of the UTF-8 string `<SEED>|<purpose>|<domain>`, with **`SEED = ai-checkout/phase-12/2026-10-06`** (unchanged from `.1`). The seed is a label: its date is one day ahead of the actual UTC date of Phase 12.1 (2026-10-05), and it is kept unchanged to preserve the frozen selections. Domains are taken in ascending key order. The purposes are `pipeline-heldout`, `reader-candidates`, `reader-split`, `reader-country` (new in `.3`: the order in which countries take turns) and, for building frames 2 and 3, `retailer-family`. Every selection depends only on a domain's band, eligibility, region group, home list, family and probe status in the frame and on keys, never on an exact rank.

The procedure is implemented in `evals/merchants/tools/seeded-selection.mjs` (SHA-256 `938bee15e3b9fc2854639fb26e52bc802953d796da4e5cfb2e6789d0c7c75052` at `.3`; `d8f19e0dbd2aac692a516c94d7b15897e3b0bd2ea5d1df424cb8c91b56073c5f` at `.2`), which has no network access and no randomness. Its tests (`seeded-selection.test.mjs`, run by `npm run test:scripts`) cover frame 2 against frame 1, frame 3's buckets, regions, currencies, operators, entry hosts and one storefront per family per country, the minor-unit table and item price bands against frame 3, both candidate streams (bucket order, key order, the non-U.S. round robin), the unchanged held-out list, and the split's balance, operator lock and refusals. `python3 evals/merchants/tools/build-retail-frame-3.py --check` rebuilds frame 3 from the CrUX copies and the committed inputs and must reproduce it byte for byte. The script and its constants MUST NOT change after the protocol is signed. A bug fix is a dated [amendment](#changes-to-this-protocol) that reports both outputs.

## Candidate sampling (applied in 12.3)

1. **Visit order, most popular first.** Each stream's eligible sites are taken bucket by bucket: every `top-1k` site before any `1k-5k` site, and so on.
   - **U.S. stream:** within a bucket, in ascending `reader-candidates` key order.
   - **Non-U.S. stream:** within a bucket, **round robin over the home countries**: the countries in ascending `reader-country` key order (SHA-256 of `<SEED>|reader-country|<CC>`) each give their next site in `reader-candidates` key order, turn after turn, a country dropping out when its bucket is used up. Round robin, not weighting by market size, because the reader's failure modes are mostly locale-driven (formats, symbols, languages), so equal turns test the most locales; and because market-size weights would need a market table outside CrUX that the builder can't fix reproducibly.
2. **Candidates (400, 200 per stream).** The first 200 sites of each stream's visit order. `seeded-selection.mjs reader-candidates` prints both lists (output SHA-256 `fd01a31f0b1daaed3846f156b4aaeeea722c9889a2cc1103107a380938b28ce7`): `us` 99 `top-1k` and 101 `1k-5k`; `non-us` 200 `top-1k` from all 24 countries, 9 each from 20 of them, SA 10, and all of ID 5, CN 4 and NG 1 (`europe` 81, `asia-pacific` 45, `middle-east-africa` 38, `canada-latam` 36), in 21 currencies. Capture MUST follow the visit order and MUST NOT skip a candidate except for a recorded exclusion. The two streams may be interleaved in any way, because each stream's stop rule counts only its own sites.
3. **Stop.** A stream's capture stops when **110 of its sites are captured**, so capture ends with at most **220 sites**, about 73 per split. A site counts as captured when its `cart-1` or `minicart-1` real snapshot exists. If a stream's 200 candidates are exhausted with fewer than **80** captured sites, the builder MUST stop and report to Evan. With his approval, that stream continues with its next sites in visit order (`--extra-us N` or `--extra-non-us N`).
4. **Probe sites.** The Phase 10 sites are no longer forced in. 17 of the 25 are U.S. candidates on their own popularity (15 in the first 110 visits; bose.com is visit 147 and yeti.com 197); barnesandnoble.com is eligible in `1k-5k` but not drawn, and the other seven are not in frame 3. Candidate probe sites are captured afresh with the Phase 12 tool; their probe labels are not reused, and they go to `development` only.
5. **Reporting.** Non-U.S. results weight **locales equally, not by market share**: each country gets about the same number of sites, so the non-U.S. Y is an average over locales, not a share of non-U.S. shopping, and every report MUST say so. Per-country figures are descriptive only, never compared with a target. For each stream, band, region group and home country, the report gives candidates visited, sites captured, the blocked share and every exclusion with its code. The top bucket will be the most blocked (the probe reached 3 of 8 top-1k carts), and the report MUST say how thin it is. The blocked sites are the [known gap](#bot-walled-sites-a-known-gap).

**Sizing.** With about 73 sites per split, each split holds about 36 U.S. and 37 non-U.S. sites. A simulated capture of the first 110 candidates of each stream gives 38 / 37 / 35 U.S. and 36 / 36 / 38 non-U.S. sites in held-out A / held-out B / development, operator lock included, with the held-out splits within one site of each other in every region group. Each held-out split should then have about 55–60 one-item cart pages for Y, about half of them outside the U.S. A non-U.S. stream that stops at 110 holds about 4–5 sites per country, so per-country results are never reported with targets.

## Capture posture

The Phase 12.2 capture tool enforces these rules; they are not left to the operator. Each refusal has a unit test.

- **No reader code during capture.** No reader, prototype reader or replay hook runs on a live page or a fresh snapshot during capture, and capture records no reader result. The first reader run on any snapshot comes after the [freeze](#freeze-and-errata) of all three splits.
- **Roles.** The Phase 13 reader developer (agent or session) MUST NOT operate the capture, write or edit recipes, or see snapshots of the held-out splits.
- **Never** sign in, create an account, type into any field, choose from a `<select>`, submit a form, place an order, solve or attempt a CAPTCHA, or press-and-hold a verification. The tool has no typing path and no coordinate-click path.
- **Clicks.** The tool refuses clicks on submit controls and on controls inside a `<form>`, except controls on the per-recipe allowlist. The allowlist may hold add-to-cart, size and colour choices that are buttons, closing a popup, declining cookies, a non-form "continue as guest" and a control that **keeps** the storefront the site served (for example "stay on this site"). Recipes are committed data. The tool never interacts with a cross-origin frame. Its local control server requires a token and checks the `Origin` header.
- **Browser.** A persistent Chrome profile for the capture only, never Evan's and never signed in to anything, stored in a gitignored path. No extension is loaded during capture. The probe's test extension may have triggered Nike's refusal. No stealth or fingerprint-evasion plugin, no proxy, no VPN, no IP rotation.
- **Pace.** One site at a time and never two sites in parallel. At least 3 s between consecutive navigations or clicks on a site. At most 25 top-level navigations per site per session. One [reconnaissance session](#reconnaissance-new-in-4) and then one capture session per site. A second session of either kind, on a later day, is allowed only after a tool failure in that kind of session and never after a block.
- **Blocks.** On a bot wall, HTTP 403 or 429, CAPTCHA or "disable your extensions" message, the tool stops that site at once and records the code. Nothing is retried, solved or bypassed.
- **Location, country and currency.** Capture runs from the operator's own connection in the United States. The storefront, country, language and currency the site serves are accepted as given: no country or currency chooser is changed, no country or locale parameter or path is edited, and a ZIP or postcode chosen by the site is never changed by typing. A site that redirects to a different registrable domain (for example a country domain) is excluded as `redirected-off-domain`. The capture records the observed storefront region and currency per site.
- **Entry host (new in `.3`).** Capture starts at the site's `entryHost` in the frame (its best-ranked storefront host in its home list, ties by the order of `hosts`). A recipe MUST name only hosts from the site's `hosts`, apart from one third-party checkout page as the tool allows; a recipe on another host of the domain is a deviation. Since `.4`, a cart or checkout page that the site itself serves on another host of the same registrable domain, reached by the site's own link or redirect (eBay's `cart.ebay.com`), may be loaded and captured; its host is recorded with the state, and its robots.txt is read before its first page (below). Listing and product URLs stay on `hosts`.
- **Cookies.** Cookie banners are declined where a decline control exists, otherwise closed or left alone.
- **Item unavailable at capture (new in `.4`).** The capture session uses exactly the item and option the reconnaissance fixed. If that item is out of stock or unavailable, its add-to-cart is refused or fails, or its fixed option can't be chosen within the posture, the site is excluded as `add-to-cart-refused` with screenshot evidence. No other item is substituted.
- **Item.** The first in-stock physical item, in the listing's order as defined under [reconnaissance](#reconnaissance-new-in-4), whose displayed price lies within the band for the storefront's currency in `evals/merchants/item-price-bands.json` (`item-price-bands.1`, SHA-256 `644f0d12213b8c5e92860e79c40dea18cef5fffc54dbb7babe656f756ead9b1c`). Each band approximates USD 10–200, from rounded rates in the builder's knowledge; the band only picks the item and never enters a label or a score. The item must not be a gift card, subscription, age-restricted item, personalised item or item needing a typed choice. The second item for `cart-2items` follows the same rule.
- **robots.txt (Evan's decision, 2026-10-06; replaces the 2026-10-05 rule).** The tool identifies itself honestly in robots.txt terms with its own product token, `AICheckoutCapture`, and applies the rules for `User-agent: *` and for that token together. Each session fetches the entry host's robots.txt before any other page load, and the robots.txt of **every other host of the site it loads** (a cart or storefront subdomain) before that host's first page, or, when a redirect lands there, before anything else happens on that page; copies stay gitignored. `sites.json` records each file's SHA-256 and the applicable rules. **A site is excluded only if those rules disallow everything on the entry host or on any host the session loads (`robots-disallow-all`).** "Disallows everything" means exactly: the root path `/` of that host is disallowed for `*` or for `AICheckoutCapture` under standard RFC 9309 evaluation (longest matching rule wins, Allow wins a tie). So `Disallow: /` excludes, also with only narrower Allow lines such as `Allow: /cart`; `Disallow: /` together with `Allow: /` or `Allow: /$` does not; `Disallow: /*` and `Disallow: /$` do. A disallowed cart, checkout or other path does **not** exclude the site: the recipe's cart and checkout paths are recorded with whether robots.txt allows them, the cart path against the rules of the host the reconnaissance loaded it on (`cartHost`, whose robots.txt the capture session reads before any page), every captured state records whether its path is allowed, and the report gives counts. Evan's reasoning: nothing wrong is done and nothing is bought. Every other safety rule is unchanged.
- **Terms (Evan's decision, 2026-10-05).** The capture loads the terms page linked from the site's footer once. `sites.json` records its URL and whether it prohibits automated access (`yes` / `no` / `unknown`), and the report gives counts. A terms clause **does not exclude** the site.
- **Exclusion codes at capture.** `blocked-bot-wall`, `blocked-http-403`, `blocked-http-429`, `captcha`, `blocked-extension-check`, `add-to-cart-refused`, `no-eligible-item`, `needs-input`, `sign-in-required`, `redirected-off-domain`, `geo-blocked`, `not-a-store`, `robots-disallow-all`, `defunct`, `tool-error` (after two sessions) and `would-need-forbidden-action`. `non-us-storefront` is retired, as a non-U.S. storefront is now in scope; `robots-disallow-path` is retired in `.4`. A reconnaissance or capture session may end with any of these codes, and the tool records stop codes itself. A block or exclusion in the reconnaissance session excludes the site, and no capture session follows. Every candidate visited gets an outcome, and the report lists every exclusion. `geo-blocked` means the site refuses U.S. visitors or U.S. delivery so that no cart can be reached ("not available in your country", a geo-block page, or a cart that refuses a U.S. visitor); it is distinct from a bot wall. The judgement exclusions `needs-input`, `no-eligible-item`, `add-to-cart-refused` and `geo-blocked` (and any `would-need-forbidden-action`) MUST carry evidence: the SHA-256 of a screenshot that shows the reason, kept with the gitignored snapshots. The independent 12.3 reviewer checks every one of them against its screenshot.

### Reconnaissance (new in `.4`)

Each site gets one **reconnaissance session** before its capture session, so that no recipe names a page nobody has seen. It runs in the capture tool, under every rule above (profile, pace, 25 navigations, robots, stops, no typing, no `<select>`, no form submission, no coordinate click, same site), and is look-only: it may only `goto`, click, wait and take `view-NN` snapshots. The tool refuses in it every action-state and `terms` snapshot, every click purpose except closing a popup and declining cookies, and any click on a control named like add-to-cart; same-site writes are aborted as in capture. Its pages are **never labelled, never page-states and never read for platform detection**, and its record (`capture-recon-record.1`, text-free) is committed beside the site record. It ends either with an exclusion (as above) or with its **findings**, which the tool accepts only if the listing, the item pages and the cart path are pages the session itself loaded, the listing and item pages are on the site's `hosts` in the frame (only the cart and checkout may be on another host of the domain), and the session took a `view-NN` snapshot of the home page and of the listing. The tool then writes the draft recipe, which names the session and the host the cart path was loaded on (`cartHost`), and commits the session's ordered navigation list (URLs without query, token-like segments masked) in its record. The capture session refuses a recipe whose origin, listing, items, cart path, cart host or checkout paths differ in any way from the findings. The operator adds only the allowlist and steps; the item and paths are fixed. **The 12.3 reviewer MUST check each recipe's listing and item against the reconnaissance snapshots and visit order**, as for judgement exclusions; a recipe that does not follow the rules below is a deviation.

The findings are decided by rules, so the operator makes no choice:

- **Listing.** A page is a *listing* when it shows at least four product tiles, each with a displayed price and a link to its own product page on the site. The candidate links are the links of the entry host's home page, in document order: first those inside its first navigation landmark (`<nav>` or `role="navigation"`, including collapsed menu links present in the DOM), then the rest. Links to sign-in, account, help, store locators, gift cards, the cart, another language or country, or another registrable domain are skipped. The operator opens the candidates in order (at most six); the first that is a listing is the listing. When an opened candidate is not a listing, its own first candidate link inside the main content (outside header, navigation and footer) is opened once before moving to the next home-page link. If none is a listing, the site is excluded as `no-eligible-item`. A search box is never used.
- **Listing order and first item.** The tiles in the listing's document order, as in the `view-NN` snapshot taken after it settles (no scrolling, no "load more"); a product shown twice counts at its first position, and tiles linking off the domain are skipped. A tile's displayed price is the current price on the tile: never a struck-through or "was" price; a range or "from" price counts as its lowest amount; a member- or loyalty-only price is ignored. The **item** is the first tile whose price lies within the band for the currency the storefront shows, that is not a gift card, subscription, age-restricted, personalised or typed-choice item, and whose product page, when opened, shows it in stock (an add-to-cart control that is enabled, or enabled after choosing the first in-stock size or colour in document order) at a price still within the band. Otherwise the next tile is taken. The second item for `cart-2items` is the next qualifying tile. A storefront currency missing from the band table is added by a dated amendment before that site's capture, as `item-price-bands.json` states.
- **Cart path.** The path of the site's own cart link in the page header (a link or control whose accessible name or label means cart, bag, basket or trolley, in the page's language): its `href`, or the page a plain click on it lands on, or, for a drawer, the path of the drawer's "view cart" link. The reconnaissance loads it once, empty.
- **Checkout paths.** The `href` of any checkout link the cart page shows; often none with an empty cart, in which case the capture session reaches `checkout-1` by clicking the cart's checkout control.

### Bot-walled sites: a known gap

Sites the tool can't reach because of a bot wall, HTTP 403 or 429, CAPTCHA or extension check are excluded with their code, as in `.1`. They are **listed and reported as a known gap**: counts and domains per stream, band and region group, and their share of the visited candidates. `geo-blocked` sites are reported in the same section but **separately** from bot walls, by stream, band and region group, since they reflect the U.S. vantage point rather than automation defences. Every reader report repeats the gap beside its results, since these are disproportionately the largest retailers.

**Deferred (Evan, 2026-10-05).** A later step may capture bot-walled sites attended, with Claude driving its built-in browser and Evan solving any CAPTCHA. That needs its own protocol amendment and an Evan-approved in-page serializer. Until then, no site is captured any other way than by the 12.2 tool, and the agent never solves or attempts a CAPTCHA or bot wall.

## Platform detection

The platform group comes from markers in the captured HTML of a fixed set of a site's states, `empty-cart` and its first captured cart state (`cart-1`, or `minicart-1` when `cart-1` was not reached), and from the host of the checkout URL its committed recipe names. Later states (checkout, quantity 2, two items), the `terms` page, operator `view-NN` pages and reconnaissance pages never change it. A script in the 12.2 tool detects it and records the matched marker. It is the only page-derived input to the split, and no person chooses it. The first group that matches wins:

| Group | Markers |
| --- | --- |
| `shopify` | `cdn.shopify.com`, `Shopify.shop`, or a checkout on `checkout.shopify.com` or a `/checkouts/` path on a Shopify-marked host |
| `sfcc` | `/on/demandware.store/`, `demandware.static`, `dwvar_` |
| `adobe-commerce` | `Magento_` module paths, `x-magento-*` headers (not `mage/`, which matched `image/` in the probe) |
| `sap-commerce` | `/_ui/` together with `ACC.` or `hybris` |
| `bigcommerce` | `cdn11.bigcommerce.com`, `bigcommerce.com/s-` |
| `other-detected` | `woocommerce`, `vtex`, Wix or Squarespace commerce markers, Oracle Commerce `atg`, Shopware (`/bundles/storefront/`), PrestaShop (`prestashop`), Cafe24 (`cafe24`), MakeShop (`makeshop`) |
| `none-detected` | none of the above. Next.js and similar frameworks are not platforms |

Shadow-DOM storefronts and third-party checkouts are [observed tags](#states), not platform groups. The probe found no cart summary in a shadow root, so splits are not required to contain one.

## Splits (defined now, applied in 12.3)

`seeded-selection.mjs split <captured.json>` assigns the captured sites. Its input is `{domain, platform}` per site and nothing else:

1. Captured probe sites go to `development`.
2. The strata are band × region group × platform group, processed in the order `top-1k`, `1k-5k`, `5k-10k`; then `us`, `canada-latam`, `europe`, `asia-pacific`, `middle-east-africa`; then `shopify`, `sfcc`, `adobe-commerce`, `sap-commerce`, `bigcommerce`, `other-detected`, `none-detected`. Within a stratum, sites go in ascending `reader-split` key order.
3. Each site goes to the split with the fewest sites in its stratum. Ties go to the split with the fewest sites in its band × region group, then in its region group, then in its band, then overall, then the first of `heldout-a`, `heldout-b`, `development`. Probe sites count in these tallies.
4. **Operator lock (new in `.3`).** A site whose operator (from the frame) already has a site placed goes to that site's split, whatever its stratum, so storefronts that share one company's store code (amazon.de and amazon.com.au; potterybarn.com and westelm.com) never sit in development and a held-out split at once. A probe site's operator therefore goes to development. Sites of one operator are correlated, and the report gives the number of operators with more than one site per split.

All non-U.S. candidates are in `top-1k`, so for the non-U.S. stream the strata are effectively region group × platform group.

With up to 220 captured sites, each split holds about 70–75, about half of them outside the U.S. The two held-out splits' counts per region group differ by at most one in the simulation, operator lock included. A stratum with at least three non-probe sites puts at least one site in each split. The assignment depends only on the frame and the platform group. It never depends on which later states were reached, on totals, currencies or anything else seen in a page; the platform group itself comes only from the fixed states named in [platform detection](#platform-detection). It MUST be computed and committed (`splits.json`) before any labeller starts. It MUST NOT be changed after that, except that a site later excluded (for example as `not-a-store`) is removed and reported, never replaced.

## States

**Action states** are produced by allowlisted actions on the live site, in this order:

| State | How it arises |
| --- | --- |
| `empty-cart` | The cart page before anything is added |
| `minicart-1` | The in-page cart (drawer, popover, dropdown or modal) right after one add-to-cart, if the site opens one |
| `cart-1` | The site's cart URL with one item at quantity 1. **The state for Y** |
| `checkout-1` | The first checkout page reachable without sign-in or input, with that one item. Shopify and Magento checkouts may be opened by URL, as in the probe |
| `cart-qty2` | The same item at quantity 2, from a second add-to-cart or an increment control the recipe allowlists. Never by typing or a `<select>` |
| `cart-2items` | A second, different item added |

A state the site can't reach within the posture is recorded as `not-reached` with its reason and is not a page-state.

**Observed states** aren't produced. Labellers tag them on real page-states, and the report shows them as subgroups with their n and without targets:

- **Page features:** `sale-strikethrough`, `promo-banner`, `price-carousel`, `installment-widget`, `free-shipping-progress`, `tax-or-shipping-estimate`, `credit-applied`, `loading-indicator`, `summary-in-open-shadow`, `summary-in-closed-shadow`, `summary-in-iframe`, `third-party-checkout-host`.
- **Locale and format (new in `.2`):** `decimal-comma` (12,99), `thousands-dot` (1.299,00), `thousands-space` (1 299,00, any space character), `thousands-apostrophe` (1'299.00), `currency-after-amount` (12,99 €), `currency-code-only` (EUR 12.99, no symbol), `zero-decimal-currency` (JPY, KRW and others whose ISO 4217 minor unit is 0), `shared-symbol` (a symbol several currencies use, such as `$`, `kr`, `¥` or `R`), `multiple-currencies-shown`, `non-latin-digits`, `rtl-layout` and `non-english-labels`.

### Offline variants

States that need input (a gift card code, a ZIP for a tax estimate), adversarial cases and locale formats are made offline from real snapshots of the same split. The transforms are deterministic and committed. Each has a version, and the variant manifest is hashed in the [freeze](#freeze-and-errata). The variant's expected label is derived from the base label by the transform's rule. Amounts a transform writes use the base page's currency and format unless the transform changes them.

| Transform | Applied to | Change | Expected label |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `class-rename` | every real `cart-1` and `checkout-1` | Every `class` and `id` value renamed through a seeded bijection; text and structure unchanged | Unchanged |
| `promo-row` | real `cart-1` with a non-null expected | A promotional row with an amount ("10 off orders over 100" style, in the page's currency) inserted inside the summary, next to the expected row | Unchanged |
| `fake-subtotal` | every real `cart-1` | A "Subtotal" row (in the page's language where its own labels give the word, otherwise English) with a different amount, inserted outside the summary in a recommendation block | Unchanged |
| `injected-instruction` | every real `cart-1` | Visible and hidden text addressed to an AI that names a false total. This targets labelling and capture agents, which read untrusted HTML. The reader has no model | Unchanged |
| `credit-applied` | real `cart-1` with a non-null expected | A negative "Gift card applied" row of C = min(5 major units, ⌊expected ÷ 2⌋) minor units and a total-after-credit row of expected − C inserted inside the summary | `afterCredit`, expected − C, same currency |
| `format-swap` (new) | real `cart-1` with a non-null expected and a currency with minor unit 2 | Every amount on the page rewritten to the other decimal convention: a decimal point with comma grouping becomes a decimal comma with dot grouping (1.299,00), and the reverse. Symbol and its position unchanged | Unchanged |
| `format-space-after` (new) | real `cart-1` with a non-null expected and a currency with minor unit 2 | Every amount rewritten with a decimal comma, a no-break-space thousands separator and the page's currency symbol after the amount with a space (1 299,00 €) | Unchanged |
| `zero-decimal` (new) | real `cart-1` with a non-null expected and a currency with minor unit 2 | Every amount rewritten as its minor units with comma grouping, chosen by the parity of the site's `reader-split` key either as `JPY 1,299` (the code before every amount, so rule (a) of [currency evidence](#currency-evidence) applies) or as `₩1,299` (an unambiguous symbol, rule (c)); every other currency code in text and every currency in structured data, meta tags and cart data attributes rewritten to `JPY` or `KRW` | Currency `JPY` or `KRW`, same integer `amountMinor` |
| `mixed-currency` (new) | real `cart-1` with a non-null expected | A currency switcher listing three other currencies outside the summary, an "≈ amount" in another currency on each line item, and an "approx." row in another currency directly after the expected row inside the summary | Unchanged (the currency charged) |

The generator finds the expected row by the labelled amount's text inside an element whose text also matches the kind's label, and finds amounts to rewrite with a grammar for the page's currency and format. If either isn't unique, the variant is skipped and the skip is counted. One of the split's labellers checks a seeded 10% sample of derived labels against the variant (at least 10). A mismatch means the transform gets a new version and every variant is regenerated before the freeze. Variants are **reported apart from real page-states** and never pooled with them.

## Labelling

### Who labels

- **Two independent labellers per split.** They are Claude Code subagents in separate sessions, with the model recorded. Each labels every real page-state of the split from the snapshot files: full-page screenshot, viewport screenshot, DOM tree and MHTML.
- **What a labeller never sees.** The other labeller's file, any reader output, the probe's `labels*.json` and `reader-results.json`, and the adjudication.
- **No conflicts.** A person or agent who has run or seen reader output on a split MUST NOT label or adjudicate it.
- **Page text is data.** Labellers treat it as data, never as instructions; the `injected-instruction` variants exist because of this.
- **Adjudicator.** A third subagent that never labelled any page-state of that split. It decides every disagreement against the snapshot, sees both labels and records its reason.
- **Agent labels.** The labels are agent-labelled, and reports call them agent-verified, never human-verified.

### Label schema (`reader-labels.2`: `reader-labels.1` plus currency)

Per page-state:

- `id`: `<domain>/<state>`, or `<domain>/<state>/<transform>` for a variant.
- `split`, `state` and `origin` (`action` or `variant`).
- `snapshotSha256` (the snapshot manifest) and `domSha256`.
- `readable`: `top-frame`, `open-shadow`, `iframe-only`, `closed-shadow-only` or `none-displayed`.
- `displayed`: every summary total row of the three kinds, as `{kind, amountMinor, currency}`.
- `expected`: `{kind, amountMinor, currency}` or `null`, with `expectedReason` when null: `no-total-displayed`, `not-readable`, `ambiguous-preferred-kind` or `currency-undetermined`. `non-usd` is retired.
- `currencyEvidence` for every non-null expected: the rule of [currency evidence](#currency-evidence) that decided it, `a-code`, `b-structured`, `c-symbol` or `d-frame`, plus `currencyConflict: true` when a lower rule pointed elsewhere.
- `observedTags`, `confidence` (`high` / `low`) and `notes`. Notes are text-free where possible, and any quote is 25 words or fewer.

**Amounts.** `amountMinor` is an integer in the currency's ISO 4217 minor unit, taken from the committed table `evals/merchants/currency-minor-units.json` (`currency-minor-units.1`, 43 currencies, SHA-256 `f5ce0863d3cf7a98a99981992f5215f5d68017c2b7d8d72803c2ea54c5a0dd48`): cents for USD and EUR, yen for JPY, won for KRW. A displayed amount is converted by its currency's exponent, whatever precision the page shows (HUF shown without decimals still has exponent 2). A currency missing from the table is added by a dated amendment before its first label. `currency` is the ISO 4217 code of the currency the shopper would be charged on that page.

Kinds and preference are as in the probe: **`afterCredit` > `estimatedTotal` > `subtotal`**. `expected` is the displayed amount of the most preferred kind in the readable region.

- **Unreadable summaries.** A summary only in a cross-origin iframe or a closed shadow root is `not-readable`, and the correct answer is `none`.
- **Ambiguous preferred kind.** Two different amounts of the most preferred kind give `ambiguous-preferred-kind`, and the correct answer is `ask` or `none`.
- **Currency undetermined.** Only when none of rules (a)–(d) below resolves the currency is `expected` null with `currency-undetermined`; the correct answer is then `ask` or `none`. An "approx." amount in another currency is never the expected row.

#### Currency evidence

The labeller decides the charged currency by the **first** rule that applies, in this order. A higher rule wins over a lower one; when a lower rule points elsewhere, the label records `currencyConflict: true`.

- **(a) Code in the summary.** An ISO 4217 code shown in the summary region with or next to the amounts (`EUR 12,99`, `12.99 CAD`, `US$` counts as `USD`). Dollar prefixes that name their country count as codes: `US$` USD, `CA$` / `C$` CAD, `A$` / `AU$` AUD, `NZ$` NZD, `MX$` MXN, `S$` SGD, `HK$` HKD, `NT$` TWD, `R$` BRL.
- **(b) Structured data on the page.** A currency in the page's structured data: JSON-LD or microdata `priceCurrency`, a currency meta tag (`og:price:currency`, `product:price:currency`), or a currency data attribute or value in the cart's own markup (for example `data-currency`, Shopify's `Shopify.currency.active`). If these disagree among themselves, rule (b) doesn't apply.
- **(c) Unambiguous symbol.** A symbol that only one of the frame's currencies uses, shown with the amounts: `€` EUR, `£` GBP (not `E£`, which is EGP), `₹` INR, `₩` KRW, `₺` TRY, `zł` PLN, `₴` UAH, `₫` VND, `₦` NGN, `₪` ILS, `฿` THB, `Kč` CZK, `Ft` HUF, `lei` RON, `₸` KZT, `৳` BDT, `Rp` IDR, `RM` MYR, `S/` PEN, `E£` EGP, `KSh` KES, `د.إ` AED, `ر.س` SAR, `Fr.` CHF, `円` JPY, `元` CNY.
- **(d) The frame's storefront currency.** For an ambiguous symbol (`$`, `¥`, `kr`, `R`, `Rs`) or no marker at all, the `currency` recorded for that domain in `retail-frame-3.json`, **unless** rule (a) or (b) applies or the page shows a conflicting marker: a code or country-named prefix for another currency anywhere on the page, or a displayed country or currency selector set to another currency. A conflicting marker makes the currency `currency-undetermined` unless (a) or (b) decides it.

The page's `lang` attribute, region-qualified (`en-CA`) or bare (`en`), **never decides** the currency, and neither does the domain's TLD on its own. Worked case: a bare `$` on a U.S. `.com` with no code, no structured currency and no conflicting marker resolves to `USD` by rule (d). The same `$` on `canadiantire.ca` resolves to `CAD` by rule (d); with `priceCurrency: USD` in its JSON-LD it is `USD` by rule (b), with a conflict recorded.
- **The probe's three conventions bind.** A sale-discounted total is `estimatedTotal`, not `afterCredit`. A "total excluding tax" row is `estimatedTotal`. A row the labeller isn't sure of is labelled with `confidence: low`, not dropped.
- **Consistency flag.** Product price × quantity is a consistency flag only.

### Agreement

The report gives, per split, the counts and rates of:

- **expected agreement:** same `expected` (kind, amount and currency), or both null with the same reason;
- **currency agreement:** the same currency on every displayed row both labellers recorded;
- **row agreement:** the same set of `displayed` rows;
- **readable agreement;**
- **per-tag agreement**, for information.

If expected agreement on a split is below 90%, the builder MUST stop before the freeze and report to Evan.

**Undetermined currencies.** The report gives `currency-undetermined` counts, and the counts per evidence rule and of `currencyConflict`, per stream and per split. If more than **10% of a split's real `cart-1` pages** are `currency-undetermined`, the builder MUST stop before the freeze and report to Evan.

### Freeze and errata

- **Freeze, all three splits at once.** In 12.3, before any reader run on **any** split, the final labels (agreed plus adjudicated), the variant manifest and the snapshot manifest of **all three** splits are committed, and `freeze.json` also records the SHA-256 of `currency-minor-units.json`, `item-price-bands.json` and `retail-frame-3.json`. Development is frozen the same way as the held-out splits. `freeze.json` records the SHA-256 of each file, the commit and the UTC time. The reader harness (Phase 13) MUST verify these hashes and refuse to score on a mismatch. A frozen label file is never edited.
- **Errata.** A label later found wrong goes in a dated erratum (`errata/<split>.json`) recording:
  - the old and new label;
  - the snapshot evidence;
  - who found it;
  - whether reader output triggered it;
  - the adjudicator's decision. That adjudicator has not seen reader output on the split.
- **Reporting.** Every score is reported on the frozen labels, with the score on corrected labels beside it. Pass or fail against criterion 1 is decided on the **frozen** labels. A result that passes only on corrected labels is reported as such, and Evan decides.

## Scoring

The reader answers `found` (amount, kind and currency), `ask` (one or more candidate amounts, each with a currency) or `none`. Let E be the label's `expected`.

| Outcome | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| **found-correct** | `found`, E is not null, the returned currency equals E's currency, the returned amount equals the labelled amount of the returned kind (compared as `amountMinor`), and that kind is E's kind (the most preferred present) |
| **false found** | `found` and not found-correct. This includes any `found` when E is null for any reason, and the right amount with a **wrong currency** |
| **ask** | `ask`. Sub-count: E's amount and currency are among the candidates |
| **none-missed** | `none` and E is not null |
| **none-correct** | `none` and E is null |

**Bounds.** Every proportion is reported with a Wilson 95% interval (z = 1.96). Every zero count is also reported with the rule-of-three upper bound 3/n. Both are given at two levels:

- **Page-state level:** n = scored real page-states.
- **Site-cluster level:** n = sites. A site counts as failing if any of its scored page-states fails. Since `.3` the report also gives the site-cluster bounds with sites clustered **by operator** (n = operators; an operator fails if any of its sites fails), beside the per-site ones; the pass bar is unchanged.

With about 73 sites, 0 false found gives a site-level upper bound of 4.1% (rule of three) or 5.0% (Wilson).

**Subgroups.** Every outcome count is also reported, with its bounds, for the `us` and `non-us` streams (frame region), for USD and non-USD labels (`expected.currency`), per region group, per currency with at least 5 sites and per locale tag. Subgroups have no targets. With about 37 sites, a subgroup's site-level zero bound is 8.1% (rule of three), and the report MUST say so.

**Reading time.** The reader runs in Playwright's Chrome over the replay format of 12.2. That format MUST preserve open shadow roots and the styles the reader reads. The Chrome version and machine are recorded. A *read* is one call of the reader's page-reading function on a loaded page, timed as wall time; page load is not timed. The stability check makes two such reads 500 ms apart: each is timed as its own read, and the 500 ms wait between them is not counted. Each real page-state is loaded once, given one untimed warm-up read, then read three times with each read timed. The p95 is taken over every timed read on the split.

## Pass bar (criterion 1)

On the **active held-out split as a whole** (U.S. and non-U.S. sites together), over real page-states, scored against the frozen labels:

1. **0 false found** across all real page-states and states, wrong currencies included. The report gives the page-state and site-cluster upper bounds.
2. **found-correct ≥ 80% (Y)** on the Y denominator below. This is the point estimate, reported with its Wilson interval.
3. **p95 reading time ≤ 50 ms.**

The bar applies to the whole split, not to any subgroup. U.S. and non-U.S. results are always reported separately beside it. **A whole-split pass says nothing about either stream alone**: for example about 95% found-correct on U.S. pages and about 65% on non-U.S. pages can pass Y together. Each stream's Y denominator is about 29 pages, where an estimate of exactly 80% has a Wilson 95% lower bound of about 62%, and every report MUST say so beside the per-stream numbers.

**Y denominator.** The real `cart-1` page-states of the split whose E is not null. In it, `ask`, `none` and false found all count as misses. Excluded from it, and reported with their count and share of the split's sites: sites whose `cart-1` was `not-reached` (including sites with only a mini-cart), and `cart-1` pages whose E is null (`not-readable` iframe or closed-shadow summaries, `ambiguous-preferred-kind`, `no-total-displayed`, `currency-undetermined`). Those excluded pages still count toward the false-found bar. The report gives n.

**How strict this is.** With n ≈ 58, a point estimate of exactly 80% has a Wilson 95% lower bound of about 68%, so passing Y shows the reader is good, not that its true rate is above 80%. Zero false found over about 350 real page-states is strict: a reader whose true false-found rate is 1% per page-state passes with probability of about 3% (0.99^350).

A held-out run fails if any of the three fails. **Criterion 1 is met by a specific reader commit**, recorded in `runs.json`. A reader changed after that commit (other than a change with no effect on the read path, for example a comment) and before Release A MUST pass again on the active held-out split within that split's remaining runs. Variant results are reported beside the bar, per transform, with bounds and every false found listed by class, but they aren't part of it. Results on development are never criterion-1 evidence.

## Peek policy and stop rule

- **Tuning.** Readers are tuned on `development` only. A reader developer MUST NOT open held-out snapshots, screenshots, labels or per-page outputs. They also MUST NOT browse a held-out site's product, cart or checkout pages.
- **Who runs held-out.** An evaluation subagent runs the held-out splits, never the reader's developer. The coordinator sees aggregate held-out results only: the metrics, bounds and the class-only failure counts below, never per-page outputs. Every reader run on any split is logged in `runs.json` before its results are read. The log records the run ID, UTC time, reader commit, split, frozen label hash and aggregate metrics. It is text-free.
- **No answer lookup.** A reader MUST NOT embed, read or derive any per-domain list from `retail-frame-3.json`, `retail-frame-2.json`, `retail-frame.json`, the frame inputs (`frame-2-inputs/`, `frame-3-inputs/`), the CrUX or Tranco copies, the labels or the candidate lists (for example a domain→currency map). Labelling rule (d) takes an ambiguous symbol's currency from the frame, so such a list would look up answers for held-out domains. The reader may use the page's content and its URL, including the TLD.
- **Run limit.** `heldout-a` runs **at most twice in Phases 10–17 in total**. A run counts from the moment reader code executes over any held-out snapshot, whatever the run's purpose.
- **Class-only analysis.** Failures on a held-out split are analysed **by class only**. An analyst subagent that is not the reader developer reads the per-page outputs, which stay gitignored. It returns counts per class and per stream, with no domain, amount, selector or page text. The classes are: `region-missed`, `region-wrong`, `distractor-accepted` (with the distractor: strikethrough, installment, carousel, line item, savings, shipping progress, outside summary, other-currency approximation), `kind-wrong`, `amount-parse` (with the format: decimal comma, thousands separator, symbol position, zero-decimal, other), `currency` (wrong or missing currency), `unreadable-not-none`, `tie-not-ask`, `stability`, `crash`, `over-budget` and `other`. Fixes are made and tested on development.
- **Retiring A.** A second failed run of `heldout-a` retires it. A retired split may then be used as development data. `heldout-b` becomes active under the same rule: at most two runs, failures by class only.
- **Stop rule.** If `heldout-b` also fails twice, no held-out split is left. Reader work stops, and **Evan re-decides scope before Phase 15**.
- **After a pass.** Once a split passes, further runs on it still count toward its limit.

## Merchant-pipeline held-out domains

`evals/merchants/pipeline-heldout-domains.json` (`merchant-pipeline-heldout.1`) is frozen with **SHA-256 `6bc92515c90009b623c4de3d6f78395b860bc4ee68e305a05be243229b8d79b2`** and is **unchanged by `.2` and `.3`**. It is still drawn from `retail-frame.1`, and `seeded-selection.mjs pipeline-heldout --check` still reproduces it byte for byte. Like the frames, it carries bands only, not ranks. It holds 60 domains, 6 / 27 / 27 per band. Per band, the eligible frame 1 domains are taken in ascending `pipeline-heldout` key order. The top band takes 6, a third of its 18 eligible domains, so that two thirds stay available for pipeline development. The other 54 are split evenly between the other two bands.

**Why it stays U.S.-only.** The merchant pipeline (Phase 16) drafts merchant profiles and the category evidence that card recommendations rest on, and recommendations stay U.S. and USD for now. Card recommendations for non-USD purchases (currency conversion, foreign transaction fees and non-U.S. category coding) are a later phase. A non-U.S. held-out list now would measure a pipeline output nothing uses yet. When non-USD recommendations start, that phase freezes its own non-U.S. held-out list from the reader frame then in force (`retail-frame.3` now) before any drafting.

- **Profiles.** The held-out profiles for these domains are drafted and adjudicated at the **start of Phase 16, before any drafting** by the pipeline, under the Phase 16 protocol (which needs D5), and are frozen and hashed there. A profile covers name, hosts, `hostKind`, plausible categories with evidence class and source, `brandIds` and any MCC.
- **No tuning.** No prompt, few-shot example, convention, rule or tool of the merchant pipeline may be written or tuned using these domains, their pages or their profiles. Pipeline development uses other frame domains.
- **Phase 14 seed rows.** Phase 14 may seed merchant-database rows for some of these domains (default category, brand links). The Phase 16 drafting step MUST NOT read those rows, and the Phase 16 protocol says how they are scored.
- **Replacement.** A domain found at adjudication not to be an eligible store is dropped and replaced by the next domain of the same band in `pipeline-heldout` key order. Both are recorded with evidence, and an independent subagent that is neither the adjudicator nor a pipeline developer checks every drop before the profiles are frozen.
- **Overlap with the reader splits is allowed.** The two evaluations measure different outputs from different inputs: the reader turns a page DOM into an amount and currency, and the pipeline turns public evidence into a profile and category. Their selections use independent key purposes, so neither constrains the other. Excluding these domains from reader sampling would remove a third of the 18 eligible U.S. top-1k sites from a band the probe showed is already thin. All 60 are eligible U.S. domains in frame 2; 38 are eligible in frame 3, and 26 of them are reader candidates under `.3` (37 under `.2`, 46 under `.1`). 6 are probe sites (apple, barnesandnoble, gap, homedepot, nike, target), which only ever had cart totals labelled; 5 of them are `.3` candidates. Reader snapshots and labels of these domains MUST NOT be used to develop the merchant pipeline. Both reports give the overlap.
