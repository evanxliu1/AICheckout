# AI Checkout extension

React/TypeScript, Manifest V3, Vite/CRXJS, Tailwind and Zod. A badge on the cart or checkout page of any https store shows which of the cards you own earns the most; the popup compares any purchase. The comparison runs on the device over published card terms; no account, no model at checkout.

**Status:** version 2.1.0, the Chrome Web Store release candidate (Release A, [plan](../wiki/product/phase-15-release-a.md)); the listing is not published yet. Development record: [extension](../wiki/system/extension.md), [cart badge](../wiki/system/cart-badge.md). Public pages: [privacy policy](../docs/release/privacy-policy.md), [support](../docs/release/support.md), [store listing](../docs/release/store-listing.md).

## Setup and use

Use Node 24 and npm. From the repository root:

```sh
npm ci
npm run build --workspace=ai-checkout-extension
```

Open `chrome://extensions`, enable Developer mode, and load `extension/dist/` as an unpacked extension. After rebuilding, reload the extension there before retesting; an existing unpacked profile can retain the earlier background worker.

On install a setup tab opens with the same card editor as the popup's **Edit cards**: find each card you own under **Add a card** (type part of the name or issuer; the 178 bundled cards are grouped by issuer, up to 20 per wallet), pick your default card, and **Save cards**. Only for the cards that need them, the editor then asks: **Card options** (bonus categories you chose with the bank, prefilled with the card's default; "Not sure" shows a range), **About you** (memberships and statuses, asked once for all cards), **Bonus limits** (spend so far toward a capped bonus and whether an enroll-once bonus is active) and **Point values** (each points program's published estimate, issuer-stated value or "No published value"; you can set your own value in cents per point). In builds with usage reporting (below) a consent screen follows: **Share** or **Not now**, equally prominent. No passphrase is needed; **Settings → Protect with a passphrase** encrypts saved data optionally.

**Automatic cart badge.** On every https page the packaged content script first checks the address and title for a cart or checkout word; a page with neither is left alone (nothing read, no DOM observer; only same-document navigations are listened for, to check a new address). On a cart or checkout page it runs the generic cart reader (`packages/cart-reader`): the detector confirms a cart from headings and structure, the reader finds the order summary in the visible text and shows an amount only when it is certain of the amount and its currency (U.S. dollars); otherwise the badge shows the **rates view**, your cards ranked by rate with an optional amount field, never a guess. The store is matched in the service worker against the bundled or downloaded merchant release (more than 600 named U.S. stores with their bonus-category evidence; every other site is "Another U.S. online store"; payment processors such as PayPal get no badge). The badge, bottom-right, shows your best card and its estimated reward, or the rate; click it for every card's estimate with its rule and conditions, a payment-method selector, the amount field, **Dismiss for this tab** and **Not on this site** (per-site switches and the badge switch are also in the popup's Settings). At a store the release does not name, the panel offers **Store not recognised? Suggest it**. A shopper without saved cards sees **Pick your cards to see your best card** (opens setup) with a dismiss button beside it.

How it is isolated: the content script sends the worker only the reading (`{status, merchantId: 'generic-us-online', amountCents, kind, extractorVersion}` or a reason code) and learns only whether to show the badge; it makes no network request and carries neither the catalog nor the merchant release. The badge is an extension page (`src/badge/index.html`) in an iframe inside a closed shadow root, so page scripts cannot read card names. The worker routes messages by Chrome-provided sender (`src/background/routing.ts`): the popup and onboarding pages get the wallet API, the badge iframe only `badge:*` for its own tab and only with the nonce its content script was given for that frame, the content script only readings. In the frame, pointer clicks count only while IntersectionObserver v2 reports it fully visible. The worker keeps per tab, in session storage, the matched store, the hostname (never the path), the reading and the shopper's choices.

**Popup.** Opened on a cart it reads the tab (`activeTab` + `scripting`, the same reader injected once) and fills the amount or shows the rates view; **Read cart amount** re-reads. The Merchant select offers the tab's matched store by name and "Another U.S. online store". Confirm the eligibility assumption and **Compare my cards**; the result is an estimate or a range, with the issuer's rule, its conditions and the store's category evidence where a bonus applies. Unknown bonus eligibility or cap usage can change which card is best; update reported spend before each purchase (it becomes unknown the next day). **Report a problem** (five fixed reasons, no free text) and, at an unnamed store, **Suggest this store** send the store's domain on your tap.

**Card terms and the store list.** The bundled catalog is `2026-10-05.renewal.1` (178 cards of the ten largest U.S. issuers, verified 2026-10-05, expires 2026-11-04T00:00Z) and the bundled merchant release `2026-10-11.mcc.3`. A catalog is valid for 30 days after verification; expired terms block new comparisons until a valid release is in effect. Hosted builds (below) fetch `GET /v1/catalog` and `GET /v1/merchants` right after install, weekly (one retry a few minutes later when a fetch fails) and on **Check for updated terms**; while the install-time fetch runs and the bundled catalog has expired, the popup and badge say "Checking for updated terms…". The worker validates the bounded response, release sequence, version, dates and references before one atomic storage update, rejects rollbacks, altered or malformed releases and expired terms, and keeps the current snapshot when a request fails; valid cached terms work offline. New releases invalidate comparisons and clear reported usage whose rule changed; owned cards a release removed stay visible as unavailable until removed.

## Hosted builds and usage reporting

Set `VITE_CATALOG_API_URL=https://your-api.example/v1/catalog` in `extension/.env.local` before building; the build adds only that HTTPS origin to the host permissions (beside `https://*/*`) and uses the same origin for `/v1/merchants`, the privacy-policy and credits links and the uninstall page (`chrome.runtime.setUninstallURL(<origin>/uninstalled/)`, no parameters). `npm run build:hosted --workspace=ai-checkout-extension` builds against `https://ai-checkout-api.onrender.com` with `VITE_TELEMETRY=1`. The default build is offline: no endpoint, no refresh, no consent screen, nothing sent.

With `VITE_TELEMETRY=1` (hosted builds only; the build fails without the endpoint) the worker module `src/state/telemetry.ts` sends usage reports to `POST /v1/events` after the shopper chooses **Share**: every event is built in the worker from what it already holds, validated against the shared contract (`packages/catalog-review/src/telemetry.ts`), queued (≤ 200) and flushed every 15 minutes while any wait, at once at 50, at consent and on each tap. Every report carries a random identifier (no name, email or browsing history) and never a store name, domain or address; the two tap features send the registrable domain only, and work without an identifier when sharing is off. **Settings → Usage reports** has the **Sharing usage data** toggle (off is Stop sharing: the identifier and queue are cleared) and **Delete my data** (`POST /v1/events/delete` with the identifier, then sharing stops). The public dictionary is [`docs/telemetry/events.md`](../docs/telemetry/events.md), generated from the contract (`npm run telemetry:check` in CI), and `tests/telemetry-ui.test.tsx` checks that the consent copy names every event type and uses the random-identifier wording. Content scripts import none of this and their bundles contain no `/v1/events`.

## Verification

```sh
npm run lint
npm run typecheck
npm test
npm run build
npx playwright install chromium
npm run test:browser --workspace=ai-checkout-extension
npm run test:catalog:browser
npm run test:package:browser --workspace=ai-checkout-extension
```

Run these from the repository root. Unit and component tests cover the engine inputs, the state service and migrations, the vault, catalog and merchant refresh, the badge service and routing, the generic reader's contracts, the popup and onboarding, the first-run states and the telemetry module (nothing before consent, Stop sharing, Delete, back-off, the queue cap, a property test that no automatic event carries a host, URL or domain). The packaged Chromium suite (`e2e/`) covers the badge at synthetic stores served at real hosts (`badge-any-store.spec.ts`, `badge.spec.ts`), the popup's reads, the vault, deletion, worker shutdown and recovery, and the `https://*/*` permission set; `test:catalog:browser` builds with a re-dated (expired) bundled catalog and a stub API to check the install-time refresh, changed rules, rollback refusal and offline restart, and runs `telemetry.spec.ts` (consent, the batches' shape and headers, Delete my data, Not now sending nothing); `test:package:browser` installs the extracted ZIP in a fresh profile. Fixture pages are synthetic; live store evidence is separate ([merchant verification](../docs/verification/merchants.md), [lifecycle](../docs/verification/extension-lifecycle.md), [package](../docs/verification/release-package.md)).

The package command creates `extension/artifacts/ai-checkout-2.1.0.zip` and a SHA-256 inventory from `extension/dist`. It validates the manifest (name, description length, version, the four permissions, `https://*/*` plus at most one catalog origin, the badge content script and web-accessible resource, icons) and the allowed runtime files, then verifies the compressed contents. Build first after source changes.

## Data and permissions

- Permissions: `storage`, `activeTab`, `scripting`, `alarms`; host permissions `https://*/*` (the badge at any store) and, in hosted builds, the one catalog origin. The build generates `host_permissions`, `content_scripts` (the badge script on `https://*/*`, top frame) and the badge page's `web_accessible_resources`; `scripts/release-package.mjs` refuses anything else. Justifications as submitted: [store listing](../docs/release/store-listing.md#permission-justifications-dashboard-copy).
- Stored on the device: card identifiers and options, the badge switch and the sites turned off, purchase inputs, a manual cart snapshot (amount, kind, currency, store, time, a capture id, tab and document ids, a hash of the page address), the downloaded catalog and merchant release, and in hosted builds the usage-reporting consent, identifier and queue. Per-tab badge state lives in session storage and is cleared when the tab closes. URLs, product names, addresses, payment fields and page text are never kept.
- The worker restricts local and session storage to trusted extension contexts every time it starts, so content scripts and pages cannot read them (tested on Chromium; the manifest supports Chrome 120+).
- Manual comparisons last at most 15 minutes and cart snapshots five; reads time out after eight seconds.
- Saved state is plain local storage by default; optional passphrase protection uses passphrase-derived AES-GCM with the key only in session memory ([protection design and limits](../docs/verification/local-protection.md)). The catalog, merchant release and telemetry state are never encrypted (no wallet data).
- **Delete all local data** clears all of `chrome.storage.local` and `chrome.storage.session` (cards, settings, inputs, the downloaded releases, the session key and the usage-reporting consent, identifier and queue), with explicit confirmation. While sharing is on, the worker first deletes the reports stored under the identifier on the service, as **Delete my data** does, and clears only after a 2xx; otherwise nothing is cleared and the popup offers **Try again** or **Clear this device anyway** (reports already sent then expire after 90 days).

`.env.example` documents the optional endpoint. Never place service-role or model provider secrets in `VITE_` variables. Remaining work: [roadmap](../wiki/product/roadmap.md).
